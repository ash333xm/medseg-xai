Æbar;(l) = (1 / H) Σh=1H max(0, Ah(l) ⊙ ∇Ah(l) L)
R(l) = R(l-1) + Æbar;(l) · R(l-1)}, with base condition R(0) = IN×N
Where Ah(l) is the multi-head attention map, ∇ L is the gradient of the lesion segmentation loss, ⊙ is Hadamard elementwise multiplication, and max(0, ·) is ReLU activation isolating positive relevance.

MedSeg-XAI: Auditable Transformer Relevance Explainability and Model Parameter Randomization Testing for Foundation Medical Image Segmentation

Ayush Kumar1, Niyati Shah1, and the MedSeg-XAI Collaboration
Department of Computational Radiology & Biomedical Engineering
Abstract— Foundation segmentation models such as MedSAM-2 and SAM 2.1 demonstrate remarkable zero-shot capability across multimodal volumetric MRI scans. However, the black-box nature of multi-scale attention mechanisms limits their adoption in high-stakes neuro-surgical planning. Naive attention maps are known to suffer from background token noise and fail basic faithfulness sanity checks. In this work, we introduce MedSeg-XAI, an auditable clinical segmentation and explainability pipeline. MedSeg-XAI couples 3D volumetric prompt propagation with Transformer Multi-Modal Relevance Explainability (TMME), which computes gradient-weighted, ReLU-filtered relevance rollout across encoder self-attention, video memory attention, and mask decoder cross-attention layers. To ensure clinical safety, we integrate an 8-stage cascading Model Parameter Randomization Test (MPRT) as an automated clinical quality gate: segmentation masks are always presented, but visual heatmaps are strictly withheld if the model fails parameter sensitivity checks. Evaluated on the BraTS MSD Task 01 cohort, MedSAM-2 achieves a mean 3D Whole-Tumour Dice of 0.812 ± 0.048, while TMME heatmaps cleanly pass the audit gate with Spearman rank correlation collapsing from 1.000 (control) to 0.027 (full decoder).

1. Mathematical Formulation of MedSAM-2 3D Segmentation

Let a 3D volumetric MRI scan be denoted as a sequential stream of axial slices \( \mathcal{V} = \{ x_t \}_{t=1}^T \), where each slice \( x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W} \). In our clinical pipeline for MSD Task 01 (BraTS), \( T = 155 \) slices at native resolution \( 240 \times 240 \), preprocessed using percentile clipping \( [P_{0.5}, P_{99.5}] \) and z-score intensity normalization.

The MedSAM-2 architecture processes the volume through a hierarchical Hiera vision transformer encoder, yielding multi-scale feature embeddings \( f_t = \mathcal{E}(x_t) \). Prompt conditioning is provided on key slice \( t^* \) via a bounding box prompt \( \mathcal{B} = (x_0, y_0, x_1, y_1) \). The mask decoder computes cross-attention between prompt tokens \( p \) and image features \( f_{t^*} \), producing prediction mask \( M_{t^*} \). Bidirectional volumetric propagation is achieved via the memory attention bank:

\[ m_t = \text{Softmax}\left( \frac{Q(f_t) K(\mathcal{M})^\top}{\sqrt{d}} \right) V(\mathcal{M}) \]

where \( \mathcal{M} = \{ (f_k, M_k) \}_{k \in \text{Mem}} \) encodes past and future slice predictions, preserving 3D spatial continuity.

2. Mathematical Formulation of TMME Explainability

Standard transformer attention maps \( A \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times N \times N} \) visualize raw token pairwise affinity but fail to isolate tokens actively driving the predicted segmentation mask. Following Chefer et al., we formulate TMME by weighting attention maps by the backward gradient of the target lesion score \( \mathcal{L} \):

\[ \bar{A}^{(l)} = \frac{1}{H} \sum_{h=1}^H \max\left( 0, A_h^{(l)} \odot \nabla_{A_h^{(l)}} \mathcal{L} \right) \]

where \( \nabla_{A_h^{(l)}} \mathcal{L} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial A_h^{(l)}} \), \( \odot \) represents Hadamard element-wise multiplication, and \( \max(0, \cdot) \) is a ReLU operator ensuring that only features exerting positive gradient contribution towards the tumor segmentation are retained.

To account for multi-layer depth, relevance is propagated forward from layer \( 1 \) to \( L \) using identity-initialized rollout:

\[ R^{(l)} = R^{(l-1)} + \bar{A}^{(l)} \cdot R^{(l-1)}, \quad R^{(0)} = I_{N \times N} \]

For cross-attention blocks (prompt-to-image and memory-to-image), token relevance is projected onto the 2D image grid and bilinearly upsampled to \( 512 \times 512 \), normalized to \( [0, 1] \).

3. Mathematical Formulation of MPRT Safety Auditing

To guard against spurious, edge-dependent saliency artifacts (Adebayo et al., 2018), we implement an 8-stage cascading Model Parameter Randomization Test. At each stage \( k \in \{0, \dots, 7\} \), weights of module group \( \mathcal{W}_k \) are re-initialized from a Gaussian distribution preserving empirical variance:

\[ W_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}\left( 0, \, \text{Var}(W_{\text{clean}}) \right) \]

The fidelity of the explanation is evaluated by computing the Spearman rank correlation \( \rho \) between the clean heatmap \( H_{\text{clean}} \) and scrambled heatmap \( H_{\text{rand}}^{(k)} \):

\[ \rho = 1 - \frac{6 \sum_{i=1}^P d_i^2}{P (P^2 - 1)} \]

where \( d_i = \text{rank}(H_{\text{clean}, i}) - \text{rank}(H_{\text{rand}, i}) \) over all \( P = 512 \times 512 \) pixels. The pre-registered audit decision function is:

\[ \text{Audit}(x) = \begin{cases} \text{PASS (Display Heatmap)}, & \text{if } \bar{\rho}_{\text{decoder}} \le 0.30 \land \bar{\rho}_{\text{control}} \ge 0.99 \land \bar{D}_{\text{rand}} < 0.30 \\ \text{FAIL (Withhold Heatmap)}, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

1. Safe Harbor De-Identification Protocol

All clinical DICOM studies have undergone complete automated redaction of all eighteen (18) Protected Health Information (PHI) identifiers:
  • Names, geographic subdivisions smaller than a state, and residential addresses.
  • All dates directly related to an individual (birth, admission, discharge, death).
  • Telephone numbers, fax numbers, and electronic mail addresses.
  • Social Security numbers, medical record numbers, and health plan beneficiary identifiers.
  • Account numbers, certificate/license numbers, and vehicle identifiers.
  • Device identifiers, serial numbers, Web URLs, and IP addresses.
  • Biometric identifiers and full-face photographic images.
  • Any other unique identifying number, characteristic, or code.

2. Zero-VRAM PACS Isolation Architecture

- No Cloud Data Transmission: No slice or patient pixel array is transmitted across external networks. All computations are statically cached in local secure memory.
- Stateless Client Viewer: The interface executes purely as a stateless client viewer. Patient data is cleared from volatile memory upon session termination.
- Zero Model Mutation: Weights are read-only; no backpropagation or parameter updates occur during audit.
